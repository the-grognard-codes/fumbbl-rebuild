- Reading text and pixel controls have distinct roles. Computed flat-color

## Priority issues

### 1. P1 — The entry pages describe the project more than the game

**What:** The homepage leads with “Educational project,” “client,” and “future
game-service boundary.” Sign-in headlines “Use an environment-specific account.”

**Why:** Coaches must translate development language before understanding what
they can do. This conflicts with the already-confirmed product direction.

**Fix:** Lead with building teams, playing human/computer opponents, and watching
matches. Move learning history to the planned development area. Use a plain
sign-in heading, with relevant DEV/account-separation information secondary.
Keep independence statements and avoid unverified release claims.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.

### 2. P1 — Sign-in lacks clear waiting and recovery states

**What:** Async sign-in handlers provide no pending announcement or button
guard. Generic errors offer no next step. If initialization fails, handlers
are not registered, but controls remain enabled. The sign-in page also lacks
a direct Support link.

**Why:** A coach cannot distinguish waiting from failure or find an obvious way
to recover. Enabled controls can misrepresent whether a method is available.
The live Google chooser appeared and the owner completed sign-in successfully.
The source-backed failure paths above were not reproduced; this finding concerns
feedback and recovery, not an observed production outage. The ordinary loaded
PROD message is configuration context, not a request-specific pending indicator.

**Fix:** Announce pending requests, guard repeated submission, retain the email,
and offer safe retry/recovery guidance. Disable unavailable methods with a
useful explanation and an accessible help route.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`.

Source: [sign-in handlers](C:/Users/jaken/Git-Hub/fumbbl-rebuild/site/src/assets/login.js:16).

### 3. P2 — Support is a device reference before it is a help destination

**What:** Contact appears after viewing targets, scaling calculations, controls,
and future hardware planning. Live inspection confirmed that order and the lack
of opening task shortcuts. The existing layout-fit disclosure and calculator work.

**Why:** Someone who cannot sign in must work through unrelated device guidance
to find help. The content is useful, but its order does not match that task.

**Fix:** Open with routes for sign-in trouble, screen/input guidance, and contact.
Use anchors and progressive disclosure for detailed calculations and hardware
reference material. Keep the existing qualifications, working calculator,
labels, status updates, keyboard behavior, and layout-fit disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`.

Source: [support contact](C:/Users/jaken/Git-Hub/fumbbl-rebuild/site/src/support/index.html:77).

### 4. P2 — Small pixel labels and navigation hit areas need a legibility pass

**What:** Navigation is approximately 9.92px with a 16.86px text line box and no
link padding. Primary button text is approximately 10.88px. The buttons themselves
are about 49px high; small labels and small hit areas are different problems.
These dimensions come from the source/computed-style evidence, not new live
pixel measurement. The desktop live review does not replace the earlier 390px
capture or establish phone touch usability.

**Why:** The phone capture shows closely packed navigation rows. The approved
font can remain while its readable size and clickable area improve.

**Fix:** Test a modestly larger label scale and roomier navigation hit areas,
targeting 44px touch controls. Verify wrapping at 390px and preserve current
font roles. This review does not establish a WCAG failure or physical-device result.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`, followed by focused typography review.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

The initial independent checklist found **3/8 failures: moderate load**—chunking, working
memory, and progressive disclosure. Support asks readers to combine CSS pixels,
display scale, browser zoom, and available browser space while most advanced
material remains expanded. Its existing layout-fit disclosure works and the
calculator reduces arithmetic effort; the remaining concern is the volume and
ordering of reference material before contact help. Grouping and local action
hierarchy are sound. The provisional 23/40 score is retained; verification alone
does not represent an implemented design improvement.

The five header links are ordinary navigation; they are not automatically an
excessive-choice defect. Likewise, five ordered slider values are one scalar
choice, not five competing tasks.

The logo and pitch create a welcoming arrival. Engineering copy then creates
doubt about the product's purpose; sign-in terminology and weak failure guidance
can deepen that uncertainty. Clear task language, pending feedback, and an easy
help path would give the journey a more reassuring finish.

## Persona red flags

- **First-time MUTP coach:** knows BB2025 but should not need to understand
  deployment environments or game-service architecture to enter a match.
- **Distracted phone visitor:** gets a readable stacked page, but small nav
  targets and a long support path make quick entry/help harder.
- **Keyboard/screen-reader user:** labels and status regions are encouraging;
  the support slider's Right Arrow and Home behavior and visible focus were
  verified. Complete keyboard journeys, actual screen-reader announcements,
  and authentication pending announcements remain unverified.

## Minor observations and questions to consider

Support's “Find your space on the pitch” title needs a practical help subtitle.
Preserve factual support/device limits while simplifying the route to them.
The existing palette is not the reason these entry pages feel unfinished.

What should an experienced coach understand within five seconds? What is the
best first action: play, build a team, or watch a match?
