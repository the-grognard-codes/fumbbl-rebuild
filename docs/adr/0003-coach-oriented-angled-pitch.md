## What changes

The supplied reference suggests an elevated, end-on **perspective** view: the far side is narrower than the near side. This is a visual interpretation, not a claim about Blood Bowl 3's camera internals. The owner's clarification retains this perspective composition: “parallel” describes pitch and sideline alignment in the world, rather than parallel lines on screen. The intervening orthographic v3 study interpreted that request incorrectly and is historical evidence, not the accepted projection. Strict isometric projection keeps parallel grid lines parallel, uses equal foreshortening along its three axes, and views the pitch diagonally; it is one comparison candidate, not the name or requirement for the chosen direction. Here, **2.5D** describes a projected ground plane with upright 2D sprites, without requiring 3D player models.

This decision changes the default presentation from the overhead layout described in the [existing pitch layout](../../browser-client/pitch-layout.md) to 40-degree perspective, while preserving a functional top-down tactical mode. Both modes orient the pitch length north–south and allow scrolling. The Full 26 × 15 option is removed from the current viewer and gallery; complete-pitch captures remain internal geometry evidence. The existing overhead preview and historical parity evidence remain reference fixtures. It retains the [2026-09-25 DOM/SVG renderer selection](../../.notes/overhaul-analysis/04-technology-and-decisions.md#adr-002-typescript-react-dom-interface-pixijs-board-vite), which remains conditional on integrated acceptance. Previous overhead checks do not establish acceptance for the new projection or view switching.

## Camera and interaction contract

- Preserve the canonical 26-by-15 pitch: **390 squares**, including one-square end zones at `x=0` and `x=25`, midfield at the boundary `x=13`, and 4/7/4 wide/central/wide divisions at boundaries `y=4` and `y=11`. Canonical `x` runs along the 26-square length; `y` runs across the 15-square width. The visible screen axes will differ from these coordinates. These dimensions also apply to every reference mockup.
- Project the center of a player's canonical square `(x+0.5,y+0.5)` once. In perspective, place a standing sprite's visible feet at that point; upright body overhang does not change occupancy. In top-down tactical mode, center the standing artwork's alpha bounds horizontally and vertically at that point so the body sits centrally within its square. Prone/stunned ground poses use their recorded ground-center anchor in either view. Transparent export padding must not affect placement. Grid cells, canonical player centers, selection, ground shadows, ball and overlays use the same projection and scene in every comparison; the sprite's visual anchor varies by projection. Do not estimate placement separately in generated artwork.
- Supply two opposing camera orientations, 180 degrees apart around the ground plane. Under the current canonical contract, home looks from `x=0` toward `x=25`, and away from `x=25` toward `x=0`. A real opposite viewpoint reverses both longitudinal and transverse orientation; it is not a horizontal mirror of the finished image. Keep text, numbers, and sprites upright.
- Use 40-degree end-on perspective at zero yaw as the default. Touchlines, walkway boundaries and crowd rails must share the same longitudinal world axis and screen vanishing point. Keep height, elevation, focal length/field of view and viewport framing fixed while moving along the field; do not refit the camera to remaining pitch corners on each scroll. Near squares are larger than far squares, while equal distances from the viewer give equal sizes at every camera position. The 35- and 55-degree presets and classical diagonal isometric framing remain comparisons. Variable yaw remains a later enhancement; the +/-15-degree reference images document its trade-offs without making adjustable yaw an initial-delivery requirement.
- Choose the initial orientation from persisted match membership, not the acting team, and do not flip on turn changes, touchdowns, or halftime merely because those events occur. The current browser contract keeps home and away in canonical home orientation even when the native engine changes orientation. If that contract later exposes changing defended ends, use authoritative end assignments rather than guessing them from the half number. Spectators and replay viewers can choose either end.
- Camera elevation, projection, zoom, and longitudinal position are local presentation state. They cannot change actor permissions, square identities, legal actions, command IDs, match checkpoints, or transcript records. Project canonical data for display and invert the local projection for input. Send canonical coordinates and server-issued action IDs through the existing command path; the server adapter already owns native away-coordinate conversion.
- Default **Width fit** keeps both touchlines visible across the usable viewport after reserving space for HUD and sprite overhang. Choose a lens that fits the widest visible row, then retain it while travelling; distant rows naturally narrow. The finite pitch length visible in that frame changes as the camera approaches either end. Keep touchlines, walkway and crowd boundaries registered to the turf. This is a desktop framing target, not a guarantee of readable width fit on every narrow screen or browser zoom level. Explicit detail zoom can trade width coverage for size.
- Travel along the **entire world** in either direction, with bounds that expose both end zones and their standing players. Turf texture, pitch geometry, markings, players, ball, shadows, sidelines, rails, stands, banners and torches use the same active camera. In perspective, reproject their positions and apparent sizes as distance changes; a common 2D translation is insufficient. In top-down mode, the whole world translates at constant scale. The owner explicitly selected moving crowd and stands. A stationary stadium/grass backdrop under moving squares is not acceptable. Only the HUD stays in screen space. Dragging the camera must not place a reserve, choose a destination, or commit an action. Provide keyboard pan, a return-to-action control, and explicit reveal of an offscreen selected player, target, ball, or pending decision. Avoid continuous forced camera tracking; retain user framing and respect reduced motion.
- Preserve a selectable **top-down view** at 90 degrees, using orthographic projection: no vanishing point, square ground cells and parallel pitch/sideline edges. It retains full-width framing, north–south lengthwise scrolling and the current coach orientation. Switching between perspective and top-down retains the canonical longitudinal camera position, selected player, ball, pending decision and match state. Both views support the same inspection and gameplay inputs through canonical coordinates. Top-down retains the scrolling width-fit camera; do not expose a separate **Full 26 × 15** view option. An optional minimap can supplement these views.

## Feasibility with the selected renderers

**DOM/SVG is sufficient for the first implementation candidate.** Orthographic/oblique ground geometry can use SVG affine transforms. Perspective requires projecting each square's corners and route points into SVG polygons/paths (or a separately validated projective surface), rather than assuming a six-value SVG transform adds perspective. Position existing DOM player controls at projected square centers in an unwarped sprite layer, using foot anchors in perspective and visible-artwork center anchors in top-down mode; keep HUD, menus, badges, and readable labels in screen space. These are architectural inferences from the browser primitives, not a measured implementation result. [SVG transforms](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Attribute/transform), [SVG polygons](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Element/polygon).

The v4 reference demonstrates projected SVG cells/actors and native CSS scenery planes under one travelling camera, with perspective and orthographic presets. Its Chrome checks retain all 390 cells and 22 centered actors across eight views, keep camera settings fixed through 40 camera positions, and measure scenery registration within 0.001px. Top-down checks also verify square cells, parallel edges and distance-independent scale. This establishes the reference camera's geometric feasibility with the current browser primitives; it does not establish integrated gameplay, cross-browser or foreground-performance acceptance.

The starting [`LivePitch.tsx`](../../browser-client/src/LivePitch.tsx) placed markers and routes on a rectangular grid, mapped input from an axis-aligned bounding rectangle, and ordered players by canonical rows. The integration replaces those assumptions with one projection service shared by ground rendering, sprites, overlays, and targeting. Browser-to-SVG viewport conversion is only one step: a perspective ground projection also needs its own inverse or projected-square hit test. A DOM bounding rectangle alone cannot identify a square on the angled plane. [SVG viewport matrix](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SVGGraphicsElement/getScreenCTM).

The 2026-10-04 integration confirms the DOM/SVG choice for this MVP. [`PitchProjection`](../../browser-client/src/pitch-projection.ts) owns the canonical camera and targeting math; live coaches, spectators and match replay share the new scene. The [real-server acceptance report](../specs/coach-oriented-match-ui-acceptance.md) records full Human/Orc matches, opposite coach views, tactical view, travelling scenery, viewport/zoom checks and bounded foreground observations. The [integration outcome](../specs/coach-oriented-match-ui-outcome.md) maps the delivered behavior to the approved spec. These observations do not establish sustained animation performance or remove the painted scenery's documented depth/repetition limitations.

**PixiJS remains a viable alternative**, already pinned to 8.20.1 in [`package.json`](../../browser-client/package.json). Its mesh primitives, including `PerspectiveMesh` present in the installed package, can project a pitch texture while upright sprites use the same projected anchors and camera-depth order. It still needs application-owned camera math, correct targeting, and the DOM controls/text companion. It does not supply a complete game camera or remove the sprite-readability trade-offs. [PixiJS mesh guide](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/scene-objects/mesh).

Do the initial study in DOM/SVG. Reopen the renderer selection only for a demonstrated visual, interaction, or foreground-performance blocker that survives reasonable fixes. Neither a GPU renderer nor a full 3D engine is required by the requested view.

## Integration acceptance and consequences

- Validate canonical-square-to-screen-to-canonical-square round trips in both orientations and both projections across camera settings. Exercise square centers, touchlines, end zones, boundaries, viewport resizing, pan extremes, and detail zoom. Verify switching views preserves camera position, selection, pending decisions and command identity. Reject input outside the pitch. Test sprite overhang and overlapping controls separately so foreground art cannot silently redirect a square or player intent.
- Verify setup drag/drop, movement paths, waypoints, push choices, kickoff targets, player inspection, and confirmation through the existing authoritative action path. Two clients with opposite cameras must address the same canonical destination/action and converge on the same state. Reconnect and replay must preserve identities while allowing independent camera preferences.
- Retain native keyboard controls and the [pitch text companion](../../browser-client/src/PitchCompanion.tsx). Define camera-relative navigation explicitly while announcing canonical coordinates; offscreen keyboard focus must reveal its target. Verify observed screen-reader behavior, reduced motion, missing-art fallbacks, and the existing integrated browser/accessibility matrix.
- Measure crowded movement, overlay updates, and pan/zoom in a foreground browser on declared hardware. Record input responsiveness, frame-time distribution, and memory trend. The old overhead/headless parity timings cannot establish this view's performance.

This pivot increases camera, hit-testing, occlusion, and sprite-art work. The default gives a more distinctive match presentation with controlled longitudinal navigation; top-down mode preserves tactical inspection for crowded scrums, with the same north–south scrolling. It does not require changing Java rules, the browser protocol's canonical coordinates, or durable replay storage. The current reference renderer establishes exact projected geometry, centered top-down artwork and perspective foot placement, registration of the restored scenery, and invariant camera settings during travel with a fixed HUD. Reuse the approved painted scenery for MVP, recording repeated plates and illustration-plane distortion as limitations; future stadium assets should provide world coverage and upright depth-bearing crowd/stands. Implement and verify the integrated interaction seam before replacing the live board. The [full integration specification](../specs/coach-oriented-playable-match-ui.md) requires complete real-server local Human-versus-Orc play and continuity evidence; hosted signed-in testing remains a separate release gate. This ADR and its documentation captures do not establish live playability or foreground-performance acceptance.
