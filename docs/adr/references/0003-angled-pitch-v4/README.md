# Travelling MUTP pitch: perspective and top-down

The 2026-10-05 follow-up adds 30° and 50° presets for both coach ends while retaining the 40° default. Reproduce only their captures and travelling-camera checks with `node docs/adr/references/0003-angled-pitch-v4/render.mjs --follow-up`; results are saved in `geometry-follow-up.json`. At 30° the rows compress more strongly and a wider near-plane region is clipped while travelling; 50° exposes more ground between upright players. Both retain a fixed lens and height during travel.

Open the [gallery](index.html) or [reference viewer](viewer.html) directly from disk. **40° end-on perspective with zero yaw is the selected default for now.** A 90° top-down orthographic view remains available for crowded scrums. Both views retain north–south scrolling and opposing coach orientations. The 35° and 55° perspective presets remain comparisons. Match captures and near/far end captures accompany the study. Full-pitch companion PNGs remain internal geometry evidence; the Full 26 × 15 option has been removed from the viewer and gallery.

The owner's clarification means **parallel in world space, converging in screen space**. Pitch touchlines and the inner crowd/sideline rails follow the same world direction and share one vanishing point. Scrolling translates a camera along the pitch while retaining its elevation, height, lens and viewport framing. Squares shrink with distance; the same camera-relative distance gives the same apparent size at every camera position. The finite pitch portion visible in the frame changes near the ends. Width fit is chosen for the widest visible row, not recalculated from remaining pitch corners during scrolling. The capture script retains an internal full-pitch geometry overview, with scenery cropped to the finite playing surface so every square can be counted against a clear outline.

Top-down mode has square cells, parallel pitch and sideline edges, constant scale and no vanishing point. Player artwork is centered horizontally and vertically within its square using the visible alpha bounds, independently of transparent export padding. Perspective keeps the visible feet at the projected square center. It scrolls along the same north–south axis, with turf, markings, players, ball, crowd and stands moving together. The **Top-down view / Perspective view** button retains the canonical camera position and coach end; switching back restores the previous perspective preset. Top-down uses the same scrolling width-fit framing.

The earlier approved [stadium image](../0003-angled-pitch-v2/art/stadium.png), player cutouts, scoreboard and portrait/stats artwork are retained. The v3 modular stands are removed. Native CSS projective matrices register overlapping stadium-image plates to the same camera as the SVG cells and actors; a native SVG crop repeats source crowd pixels outside the original image's narrow frame. Turf, crowd, walls, flags and torches consequently move and rescale during travel. Only the HUD stays in screen space. The original source PNGs remain unchanged; [original prompts/provenance](../0003-angled-pitch-v2/art/prompts.json) remain with those assets. The HUD revision below adds a new reference-only reroll sprite.

The original illustration is a cropped perspective painting, not a complete stadium model. Repetition, blending and flattening its painted depth remain visible reference limitations, especially at the lower angles and pan extremes. This preserves the approved artwork while demonstrating travelling-camera registration. Production scenery should extend world coverage and separate upright crowd, wall, flag and torch depth without repeating or distorting the illustration. A six-value SVG affine transform cannot express this projective mapping; the viewer uses CSS projective planes for scenery and projected SVG polygons for exact game geometry.

Use wheel, drag, up/down arrows with the pitch focused, or the camera-position slider. Return to midfield restores the shared formation. Click a square for its canonical coordinates; coordinate/anchor markers are available. Dragging changes only the camera and suppresses square selection.

Checks cover every canonical cell, zone counts, centered top-down artwork, perspective foot placement, full-pitch bounds and coordinate round trips from both ends. At five camera positions for each of eight presets (40 checks) they verify invariant camera settings, equal square widths at equal camera distances, perspective pitch/rail convergence or parallel square top-down geometry, moving turf/crowd/stand registration and fixed HUD bounds. These are reference checks; live gameplay, browser zoom, accessibility and foreground performance still require integrated acceptance under ADR 0003.
